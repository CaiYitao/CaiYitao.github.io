Published

IEEE ICDM 2025 · 维也纳大学 IEEE ICDM 2025 · University of Vienna

摘要

理解基因如何被调控，是众多生物学问题的核心。实验与计算方法已经能告诉我们「哪些基因可能相互作用」，但要判断这种作用的极性——究竟是激活还是抑制——却非常困难。我们提出 Sc-GRIP（Single-cell Gene Regulation Interaction Polarity），一个图卷积框架，直接从单细胞 RNA 数据中推断转录因子（TF）与目标基因之间互作的方向性。

Sc-GRIP 把基因表达谱与基因互作图结合起来，学习基因的隐表示，再用一个双线性解码器（bilinear decoder）预测调控极性。该方法可扩展、且可针对特定细胞类型，无需依赖物种特异的先验注释或大量实验验证。

问题

转录因子是一类特殊蛋白，它们结合到 DNA 的特定序列上，决定目标基因被「打开（表达）」还是「关闭」。给定一张已知的 TF–目标基因互作图，图的结构可以从数据直接得到，但每条边的符号与权重却随细胞类型与状态而变，难以推断。Sc-GRIP 要解决三个挑战：

  1. 建模潜在的调控结构；
  2. 推断每条边的标签（激活 / 抑制）；
  3. 对生物噪声与细胞异质性保持稳健。

方法

输入是高维、稀疏的基因表达矩阵与一张无符号的 TF–目标基因邻接矩阵。Sc-GRIP 在图上做卷积、聚合邻居信号，学到每个基因的隐表示；随后用双线性解码器对每条边打分，分数的符号即对应「激活 / 抑制」。由于带标签的极性数据稀少且偏向研究充分的基因，方法采用自监督 / 弱监督的思路，使其能泛化到未见过的转录因子与细胞类型。

Abstract

Understanding how genes are regulated is fundamental to many biological questions. Experimental and computational methods can tell us which genes may interact — but finding the polarity of an interaction (whether it activates or represses) is a non-trivial problem. We introduce Sc-GRIP (Single-cell Gene Regulation Interaction Polarity), a graph convolutional framework that infers the directionality of transcription-factor (TF) gene interactions directly from single-cell RNA data.

By combining gene-expression profiles with gene-interaction graphs, Sc-GRIP learns latent gene representations and predicts regulatory polarity with a bilinear decoder. The method is scalable and cell-type-specific, and needs no species-specific prior annotations or extensive biological validation.

The problem

Transcription factors are proteins that bind specific DNA sequences and decide whether a target gene is switched ON (expressed) or OFF. Given a known TF–target graph, the structure can be read from the data, but each edge's sign and weight are context-dependent and vary across cell types and conditions. Sc-GRIP tackles three challenges:

  1. model the latent regulatory structure,
  2. infer the edge labels (activation / repression), and
  3. stay robust to biological noise and heterogeneity.

Method

The inputs are high-dimensional, sparse gene-expression matrices and an unsigned TF–target adjacency matrix. Sc-GRIP convolves over the graph, aggregating neighbour signals into a latent representation per gene; a bilinear decoder then scores each edge, and the sign of the score gives "activation / repression". Because labelled polarity data are scarce and biased toward well-studied genes, training is self- / weakly-supervised so it generalises to unseen TFs and cell types.
